How to get more Google reviews

Google reviews help people choose your business. The businesses that grow reviews fastest make asking easy, ask at the right time, and remove the friction of writing from scratch.

Why Google reviews matter

When people search for a restaurant, clinic, garage, salon, or local service, they look at your Google rating and recent reviews before they call or visit. More genuine reviews usually mean more trust, better local visibility, and a clearer signal that real customers chose you.

Getting more Google reviews is less about chasing every stranger online, and more about turning happy walk-outs into published reviews before they forget.

What actually works

If you want more Google reviews, focus on four things:

  • Ask at the right moment — right after a good experience, while the customer is still with you or just leaving.
  • Make posting easy — a QR code or direct review link beats “please search for us on Google.”
  • Help them write quickly — most people stall when they have to invent a paragraph from scratch.
  • Handle negatives privately — fix problems in WhatsApp or a call when you can, instead of only learning about them in public.

A simple step-by-step method

Step 1

Claim and clean up your Google Business Profile

Confirm your name, address, hours, category, and photos are correct. Customers should land on the right listing every time.

Step 2

Ask in person, right after a good visit

A short ask works best: “If you had a good experience, would you mind leaving a quick Google review? It helps other people find us.”

Step 3

Put a QR at the counter, table, or bill

Don’t send people hunting through Maps. A scan should open the review flow on their phone in seconds.

Step 4

Make the review feel human and fast

Give customers a few short questions about their visit, then generate a natural draft they can edit and post. Different questions each time keep reviews from sounding copy-pasted.

Step 5

Catch unhappy feedback before it goes public

If the draft is negative, route it to your team on WhatsApp first so you can resolve it. Happy customers still post on Google.

Step 6

Reply to reviews and keep asking every day

Thank people who post. Fix issues raised publicly. Train staff to ask consistently, not only when someone remembers.

Mistakes that slow review growth

  • Asking days later by email or SMS only, when the visit is already forgotten
  • Making customers search your business name manually
  • Using the same template review for everyone
  • Offering cash or gifts in ways that break Google’s review policies
  • Ignoring negative feedback until it becomes a public 1-star review

Frequently asked questions

How do I get more Google reviews? ▼
Ask happy customers at the right moment, make posting easy with a QR or direct link, help them write a natural review quickly, and follow up once. Handle unhappy feedback privately when you can.
Is it okay to ask customers for Google reviews? ▼
Yes. Asking for reviews is allowed. Do not offer incentives that violate Google policies, and never filter only positive reviews before posting.
Why do customers say they will leave a review but never do it? ▼
Usually because the process takes too long. If they must open Google Maps, find your listing, and write from scratch, most people drop off. A QR plus a ready draft removes that friction.
What should I do with negative feedback? ▼
