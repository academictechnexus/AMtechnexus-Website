How to ask customers for Google reviews

A good ask is short, timely, and easy to finish. The ask alone is not enough — customers still drop off at Maps and the blank page. NexusRevu turns “would you mind leaving a review?” into scan → 3 answers → paste on Google.

Why asking works better than waiting

Most happy customers never leave a Google review on their own. Not because they dislike you — because life moves on the moment they leave. A clear, polite ask at the right time is usually what turns a good visit into a published review.

If you want more Google reviews, the ask is half the system. The other half is making posting easy enough that “yes” becomes a published review — not a forgotten promise. See also how to get more Google reviews and where to place a review QR.

When to ask

  • Right after a good moment — after praise, a successful service, a repeat order, or a smooth checkout
  • While they are still with you — in person beats a message sent hours later
  • Once, not repeatedly — one clean ask feels helpful; three reminders feel pushy

What to say

Keep it short and specific. Examples:

  • “If you had a good experience today, would you mind leaving a quick Google review? It helps other people find us.”
  • “Glad you liked it. Scan this QR — three short questions, then a draft you can paste on Google. Takes under a minute.”
  • “Thanks for coming in. If anything was off, tell us here. If it went well, a Google review helps a lot.”

Tip: Asking for feedback first feels more honest than only asking for a five-star review. With NexusRevu, unhappy drafts can go to WhatsApp first — see how to handle negative Google reviews.

Train your team

1

Give them one script

Everyone should know the same short ask so it becomes a habit, not a special event.

2

Put the NexusRevu QR within reach

If staff have to dig for a link, they will skip the ask on busy days. One counter QR that opens the NexusRevu flow is enough.

3

Celebrate posted reviews

Share new reviews in the team chat. What gets noticed gets repeated.

What not to do

  • Do not pressure someone who is clearly unhappy
  • Do not offer cash or gifts in ways that break Google’s review policies
  • Do not ask them to invent a long review from scratch with no help
  • Do not only ask online customers and ignore walk-ins

Why NexusRevu stands out

Frequently asked questions

Is it okay to ask for Google reviews in person? ▼
Yes. A polite ask after a good experience is normal and allowed. Avoid incentives or tactics that violate Google policies.
What if customers say yes but never post? ▼
They usually drop off because writing takes effort. With NexusRevu, the next step is a QR scan and three short answers — not a manual search and a blank Google box.
Should I ask every customer? ▼
Ask when the experience went well and the moment feels right. Forced asks after bad visits create worse outcomes.
